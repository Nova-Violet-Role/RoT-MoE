# Trap corpus — pre-registration

**Written and committed BEFORE the corpus was run.** Four previous answer-quality
corpora failed their controls, and in two of them the analysis rule was chosen
after the headline was visible. `RotAbVerdict` and `RotGrounding` exist because
that went wrong twice. This document fixes every decision in advance so the
verdict cannot be steered.

## What is being tested

Whether the routed arm falls for a **trap** less often than the unrouted arm,
where a trap is an item whose obvious method yields a specific wrong answer and
whose truth is mechanically derivable.

This is **not** a test of the structural claim. That one is settled separately:
`RotDominance` is proved and `checker/dominance.sh` measures D1–D7 green on the
shipped router. `RotDominance.dominance_says_nothing_about_answer_quality` is a
theorem, so a green there may never be reported as a quality result — and,
symmetrically, a null here does not touch it.

## Corpus

- Generator: `bench/trap-prompts.js`, output `bench/trap-candidates.jsonl`.
- **n = 60**, from a pool of 71 traps; **76 non-trap items were refused** by the
  generator because `naive == truth`.
- Families: `theorem_count` 12, `guard_count` 12, `mutant_count` 36.
- Ground truth for `theorem_count` is produced by **calling**
  `checker/count-theorems.sh`, never by reimplementing its rule. That script's
  own header records the rule being copied and getting it wrong three times.
- Selection is by a property of the **item** (`naive != truth`), never by any
  arm's answer. `RotTrap.trap_selection_ignores_the_answers` proves the selected
  set is invariant under the answers;
  `RotTrap.circular_selection_cannot_lose` proves what would happen otherwise.

## Arms

| arm | meaning |
|---|---|
| `a` | unrouted — RoT MoE hooks disarmed |
| `b` | routed — the shipped nine-lens router active |

One rep per arm, same 60 prompts, same order, same model, same timeout.

## Outcome classification, fixed now

For each item, the arm's answer is parsed as the first integer in its reply.

| class | rule |
|---|---|
| `CORRECT` | integer equals `truth` |
| `TRAPPED` | integer equals `naive` |
| `OTHER` | an integer, but neither |
| `SILENT` | no integer, empty reply, or timeout |

**`SILENT` counts as incorrect. It is never dropped.** Dropping silent turns is
how an arm gets rewarded for refusing to answer.

## Primary test

McNemar exact (two-sided binomial, p = 0.5) on the **discordant pairs** of
`CORRECT`. α = 0.05.

## Power floor, fixed now

The informative band is the count of discordant pairs
(`RotTrap.band`). **If band < 10 the result is reported as `noPower`**
(`RotCeiling.noPower`) — not as a null, and not as a win. The previous corpus
returned a band of 1 out of 80; that number is why this floor exists and why it
is written down before the run rather than after.

## Deconfounding, fixed now

Both controls that killed earlier corpora are applied from the start, not bolted
on when a headline looks good:

1. **Explained-loss rule** (`RotAbVerdict`): any discordant pair where the
   losing arm was `SILENT` is marked **explained** and excluded from a second,
   deconfounded McNemar. Both p-values are reported. The headline p-value alone
   is not a result.
2. **Volume control** (`RotGrounding`): reply length in characters is recorded
   per turn. If the two arms' median reply lengths differ by more than 2×, the
   comparison is reported as **length-confounded** and the win is not claimed.

The `RotGrounding` selectivity confound is structurally absent here: every item
is generated by the same mechanical rule and neither arm chooses which items it
answers. This is stated so the absence is a reasoned claim, not an oversight.

## Decision rule, fixed now

| condition | verdict |
|---|---|
| band < 10 | `noPower` — corpus cannot decide |
| band ≥ 10, deconfounded p ≥ 0.05 | `null` — no advantage established |
| band ≥ 10, deconfounded p < 0.05, routed ahead, not length-confounded | `advantage` |
| length-confounded | `confounded` — no claim |

Any other combination is reported verbatim with no verdict attached.

## What a win here would and would not mean

It would mean: on mechanically-checkable questions of this repository where the
obvious method is wrong, the routed arm is measurably less likely to give the
obvious wrong answer.

It would **not** mean the router produces better answers in general. One corpus,
one repository, three families. That sentence is part of the pre-registration so
it cannot be forgotten if the number comes out favourable.
